Direct answer

A custom CRM makes sense when the business process does not fit a generic pipeline or when teams are forced to duplicate work across spreadsheets, chats and disconnected tools. I model the actual entities, statuses and actions first, then build the interface and integrations around them.

FAQ

Questions before the first build.

When is a custom CRM worth it?

When recurring manual work, missing roles or incompatible workflows cost more than maintaining a focused internal system.

Can it integrate with an existing website?

Yes. Website forms and product events can be connected through APIs or webhooks.

Can we migrate from spreadsheets?

Yes, after defining a stable data model and mapping the existing columns and processes.

Can it be built in stages?

Yes. Start with the core workflow and add reports, integrations and automation after real use.
